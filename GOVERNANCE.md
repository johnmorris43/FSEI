# FSEI Project Governance

## Purpose

The Flight Simulator Ecosystem Index (FSEI) is an open-source project designed to provide structured, transparent, evidence-backed analysis of the flight simulation ecosystem.

This document defines how the project is governed, how decisions are made, and how FSEI maintains its independence while encouraging participation from the flight simulation community and industry.

## Core Governance Principles

FSEI is governed according to the following principles:

- Independence
- Transparency
- Evidence-based evaluation
- Reproducibility
- Historical preservation
- Technical integrity
- Open collaboration
- Consistent treatment of products and developers

Open-source participation does not transfer control of FSEI's methodology, evaluations, roadmap, or editorial decisions to outside contributors.

## Project Maintainers

FSEI maintainers are responsible for the stewardship of the project.

Maintainer responsibilities include:

- Project architecture
- Repository management
- Release decisions
- Evaluation methodology
- Evidence standards
- Data-quality standards
- Security practices
- Contribution review
- Pull-request approval
- Project roadmap
- Governance changes

Maintainers have final responsibility for determining whether a contribution is accepted into the project.

## Decision Making

Routine technical decisions may be made through normal development and pull-request review.

Significant decisions should be documented when practical.

Examples include:

- Major domain-model changes
- Evaluation methodology changes
- Scoring changes
- Evidence-policy changes
- Breaking API changes
- Major security changes
- Changes affecting historical interpretation
- Changes to project governance

GitHub Issues, pull requests, documentation, and version history should provide a traceable record of significant decisions.

## Evaluation Independence

FSEI evaluations are independent.

No aircraft developer, simulator developer, publisher, retailer, commercial partner, sponsor, contributor, or other outside organization may directly control an FSEI evaluation or score.

Developers and other knowledgeable parties are encouraged to:

- Correct factual information
- Supply documentation
- Explain product behavior
- Clarify package structures
- Identify compatibility requirements
- Provide changelogs
- Challenge an evaluation with supporting evidence

Evaluation changes must follow the same evidence and methodology standards regardless of who requests the change.

Commercial relationships must not determine evaluation results.

## Developer and Industry Participation

FSEI welcomes participation from flight simulation developers and other industry organizations.

Developer-supplied information may be particularly valuable for establishing authoritative facts about:

- Products
- Packages
- Upgrades
- Aircraft variants
- Avionics
- Simulator compatibility
- Operating-system support
- Releases
- Pricing
- Technical capabilities

When appropriate, FSEI may identify information as developer-provided or developer-verified.

Developer verification of factual information does not imply developer approval of an FSEI evaluation.

Likewise, FSEI evaluation of a product does not imply endorsement by the product's developer.

## Partnerships

FSEI may establish relationships or partnerships with developers, organizations, communities, researchers, or other parties when those relationships advance the project's goals.

Partnerships must not provide preferential control over:

- Evaluation scores
- Scoring methodology
- Comparative results
- Evidence standards
- Competing product coverage

Where a partnership could reasonably create a perceived conflict of interest, FSEI should disclose the relationship.

FSEI should remain capable of evaluating a partner's products using the same standards applied to non-partners.

## Evidence

Important factual claims and evaluation decisions should be supported by appropriate evidence.

Evidence may include:

- Official documentation
- Product manuals
- Developer changelogs
- Official product information
- Direct testing
- Independent technical reviews
- Community reports

The strength and relevance of evidence should be considered when resolving conflicting information.

FSEI should distinguish between documented facts, direct observations, and conclusions produced by an evaluation methodology.

## Methodology

Evaluation methodologies should be documented and versioned.

A methodology change should not silently redefine historical results.

When practical, FSEI should preserve:

- Previous methodology versions
- Historical evaluations
- Evaluation dates
- Supporting evidence
- Component-level scores
- Evaluation commentary

This allows historical FSEI results to remain understandable and reproducible.

## Corrections and Challenges

FSEI welcomes corrections.

Factual errors should be corrected when reliable evidence demonstrates that the existing information is incorrect.

Evaluation challenges should identify the disputed result and provide evidence explaining why the evaluation should be reconsidered.

A developer challenging an evaluation of its own product should receive the same evidence review as any other contributor.

Likewise, criticism from a competitor or community member should not automatically be accepted without evidence.

The identity of the contributor should not substitute for the quality of the evidence.

## Conflicts of Interest

Contributors and maintainers should disclose significant conflicts of interest when those conflicts could reasonably affect an evaluation or project decision.

Examples may include:

- Employment by a developer being evaluated
- Paid consulting relationships
- Sponsorships
- Free products provided specifically for evaluation
- Commercial partnerships
- Financial interests directly related to an evaluated product

A disclosed conflict does not automatically invalidate a contribution.

Transparency allows the information to be evaluated appropriately.

## Historical Integrity

FSEI is intended to preserve the history of the flight simulation ecosystem as well as describe its current state.

Whenever practical, historical records should not be destroyed merely because circumstances have changed.

Examples include:

- Simulator generations
- Simulator releases
- Compatibility history
- Product availability
- Pricing history
- Developer activity
- Evaluation results
- Methodology versions
- Ecosystem metrics

Corrections to inaccurate historical data are appropriate.

Rewriting accurate historical data simply because current conditions differ is not.

## Open Source and Intellectual Property

FSEI is developed as an open-source project.

Contributors retain responsibility for ensuring they have the right to submit contributed material.

Open-source availability does not mean that third-party copyrighted manuals, proprietary source code, commercial assets, or other restricted material may be redistributed through the FSEI repository.

FSEI may reference external evidence without redistributing material that it does not have permission to distribute.

## Security and Privacy

Security should be treated as a project requirement rather than an afterthought.

FSEI should follow appropriate practices for:

- Authentication
- Authorization
- Input validation
- Secret management
- Dependency security
- Auditability
- Data protection
- Secure deployment

Sensitive credentials and private personal information must not be committed to the repository.

Security-related decisions may be handled privately when public disclosure would create unnecessary risk.

## Changes to Governance

This governance document may evolve as FSEI grows.

Significant governance changes should be documented through version control and, when appropriate, discussed through a GitHub Issue or pull request.

Governance should evolve deliberately rather than being changed solely to resolve a single disagreement or favor a particular contributor.

## Final Principle

FSEI exists to provide useful, explainable, evidence-backed information about the flight simulation ecosystem.

Community participation, developer expertise, partnerships, and open-source contributions can strengthen that mission.

None of those relationships should replace independent analysis, transparent methodology, or evidence.
